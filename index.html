Publications

Below are the research projects I worked on during my master’s studies. My research primarily focused on copy number variation (CNV) detection using deep learning models, with an emphasis on applying computational and machine learning approaches to genomic data. Through these projects, I explored how deep learning techniques can be used to improve the identification and analysis of CNVs and contribute to more accurate bioinformatics workflows.

1. ECOLE

ECOLE is a deep learning–based tool for detecting copy number variants (CNVs) from whole exome sequencing (WES) data. Using a transformer-based model and transfer learning from expert-labeled data, it significantly improves CNV detection accuracy compared with existing methods, achieving 68.7% precision and 49.6% recall. It can detect both germline and somatic CNVs and was also successfully applied to bladder cancer samples without requiring a matched control. The results suggest that ECOLE could make CNV detection from WES data more suitable for clinical use. (2024)

2. LYCEUM

LYCEUM is the first machine learning–based CNV caller designed for ancient DNA, enabling accurate detection despite degradation, contamination, and low sequencing coverage. Using pre-training on modern genomes and fine-tuning on ancient DNA, it also reveals patterns related to demographic history and natural selection. I presented this research at the ISMB/ECCB Conference in Liverpool, UK. (2025)

3. CHALLENGER

CHALLENGER is a masked language model–based CNV caller that uses short-read WGS data to detect variants in repetitive and challenging genomic regions, including CNVs typically detectable only with long-read sequencing. By learning from long-read, expert-labeled, and experimentally validated CNV calls, it significantly improves detection accuracy while offering a more cost-effective approach for clinical applications.
